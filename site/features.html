Features

Over 60 actions.
19 keys. Three ways to press each.

Everything below works today, free and open source. Anything still on the way is marked Coming.

Stream

Run OBS without touching the mouse

Talks to OBS directly through its built-in WebSocket server (OBS 28 and newer). No plugin to install.

Recording

Start, stop, toggle, pause and resume.

Streaming

Go live, end the stream, or start streaming and recording with one key.

Scenes & sources

Switch scenes, show or hide a source, turn filters on and off.

Replay & camera

Save the replay buffer (the "000" key is perfect for it), toggle the virtual camera.

Sound

Volume where your hand already is

System volume

Up, down, set to a level, mute. Holding the key keeps it going.

Per-app volume

Turn down the game without touching Discord or your music.

Microphone

Mute and unmute your mic, or one OBS audio source.

Media & outputs

Play/pause, next and previous track, and switch between speakers and headphones.

Apps & windows

Your computer, one key away

Open things

Apps, websites, files and folders, or your default browser.

Type & press

Type a text (with the date or time filled in), press any shortcut, copy text to the clipboard.

Windows

Bring an app to the front, close or maximise the window, switch desktops.

Screenshots & system

Full-screen or region screenshots, lock, sleep, restart and shut down (sleep and power need a second press, so nothing happens by accident).

Keys that do more

One numpad, way more than 19 buttons

Press, hold, double-tap

Three different actions on every key. A hold can also repeat (rapid-fire) after two seconds.

Layer key

Hold one key you choose, and every other key does something else, like Shift on a keyboard.

Macros

Chain steps with waits in between: switch the scene, wait three seconds, go live, show "Live!".

Modes

Turn on "Stream mode", and keys do different things while it's on. One key, two jobs.

The 000 key

That odd key on cheap numpads sends three zeros. NumpadPilot turns it into a key of its own.

Run Python Linux

For tinkerers: a few lines of Python on a key, in a locked-down sandbox.

Smart

It knows what you're doing

Per-app profiles

Your OBS keys in OBS, your editing keys in Premiere: the profile follows the app in front.

Steps aside for games

While a game with strict anti-cheat runs (Valorant, Fortnite, Call of Duty and more), NumpadPilot lets go completely, then comes back when you close it.

Unplug-proof

Pull the numpad out and plug it back in: it's picked up again by itself, with a notification either way.

Updates itself

New versions install by themselves (or just notify you, or not at all: your choice).

On-screen confirmations

A small "Recording" or "Volume 60%" pops up (a notification on Windows), so you know the key did its thing.

Your keyboard stays yours

Even a full-size keyboard with its own number pad keeps typing normally. Only the extra numpad is taken.

Private & open

It sees your keys, so it earns your trust

Collects nothing

No telemetry, no usage statistics, no account. Everything stays on your computer.

Asks before reporting

If something crashes, a plain dialog asks whether to report it, and shows you every word before anything is sent.

Open source

All of it, the settings window included, is on GitHub under the GPL-3.0, for anyone to check.

Platforms

Windows and Linux, properly

Windows 10 & 11

No driver and no administrator rights. A tray icon, start at login, and Windows' own volume pop-up.

Linux

Made on Linux, tested on KDE Plasma and Hyprland (Wayland); most actions work on any desktop. Arch, Debian/Ubuntu and Fedora installers.
