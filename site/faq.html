FAQ

Questions, answered

Something missing? Tell us here, no account needed.

Your numpad

Which numpads work?

Any USB numpad, wired or with a wireless receiver. The ones people have tested are listed on GitHub, and you can add yours with a two-minute guided test.

My keyboard already has a number pad. Can I use that?

NumpadControl is made for a separate numpad, so your main keyboard (number pad included) keeps typing normally. An old spare keyboard works as the "numpad" too.

What about the odd "000" key?

It becomes a key of its own. Saving the OBS replay buffer is a favourite.

What happens if I unplug it?

Nothing breaks: you get a notification, and it's picked up again as soon as you plug it back in.

My numpad keeps dropping off USB.

Some cheap numpads reset on a weak connection. Try a rear port on the computer, straight in and not through a hub, and another cable. The kernel log (journalctl -k) shows the disconnects if it is the hardware.

Games

Can it get me banned?

NumpadControl installs no driver. On Windows it does load a small keyboard hook into other programs, which some anti-cheat dislikes, so while a game with strict anti-cheat runs (Valorant, League of Legends, Fortnite, Call of Duty, Apex Legends, Battlefield and any game using Easy Anti-Cheat or BattlEye) it lets go of your numpad completely and takes it back when the game closes. That is the protection: it steps aside instead of promising nothing can go wrong.

Does it work in games at all, then?

In most games, yes. In the protected ones above, the numpad types normally while the game runs: that's the safe choice. You can add your own games to the list.

Privacy

It reads my keys. Does it record what I type?

No. It only acts on your numpad's keys, everything stays on your computer, and it collects nothing, so there is no telemetry and no account to make. NumpadControl is open source, so anyone can check.

What if it crashes?

A plain dialog asks whether to report it and shows exactly what would be sent. If you say yes, it goes to this website (no account needed) and appears on the Issues page; if you say no, nothing is sent.

Computers

Windows?

Windows 10 and 11, 64-bit. No driver, no administrator rights. A few apps running as administrator don't let it hide the numpad's keys from them; its actions still work there. Windows is the newer side, and some actions are still being tested.

Linux?

Yes, first-class: made on Linux and tested on Hyprland, KDE Plasma 6, XFCE and other X11 desktops, and GNOME (Wayland, with limits). Most actions work on any of them. See the docs.

Mac?

Not yet: Windows and Linux first. If you want it on a Mac, say so in an issue on GitHub.

The project

Who makes it?

An independent developer, out in the open on GitHub. No investors, no ads.

Is it really free?

Yes, and open source: every key and every action, the settings window included, with no ads and no account. Why it's free.

Can I get it now?

Yes: one command installs it, settings window included. See Get started. A Windows installer is on the Releases page; it isn't code-signed yet, and the winget package is waiting for review.
