Get started

One command, then click a key

Free and open source. The installer sets up everything, the settings window included.

Install

One command

The installer gets everything it needs and starts NumpadControl at login. After that it keeps itself up to date.

desktop_windowsWindowsterminalLinux

Open PowerShell (as your normal user, not as administrator) and paste:

irm https://raw.githubusercontent.com/youssefahmed2017/numpad-control/main/setup.ps1 | iex

Windows 10 or 11, 64-bit. It installs Python if needed. No driver and no administrator rights. Afterwards, look for the NumpadControl icon in the tray.

Prefer an installer? Download NumpadControl-Setup from the Releases page. It is not code-signed yet, so Windows SmartScreen may warn: choose More info, then Run anyway.

Open a terminal and paste:

curl -fsSL https://raw.githubusercontent.com/youssefahmed2017/numpad-control/main/setup.sh | bash

Arch, Debian/Ubuntu or Fedora (and their relatives). It asks you to press a key on your numpad to find it, and for your password once, to install packages and a rule that lets it read that numpad (and only that one).

Then

Make your first key

1
Open NumpadControl

Windows: NumpadControl Settings in the Start menu, or Settings… in the tray icon's menu. Linux: NumpadControl in your app menu.

2
Give a key an action

Click a key in the window (or press Learn and tap it on your numpad), pick an action from the list and Save. Or start from a ready-made set: ⋯ next to Profile, Add a starter preset.

3
Check your numpad

Got an unusual numpad? This walks you through every key and tells you what works, from the numpad-control folder in your home folder:

.venv/bin/python -m numpadctl check # Linux .venv\Scripts\python -m numpadctl check # Windows

Tested a numpad?
